# Originality Scout — Inspiration and Avoid Map

**Agent 02 Deliverable | Ten Agents, Ten Portraits**
**Platform:** THRIVE / Old HQ | **Date:** 2026-08-19

---

## I. The Identity We Are Designing For

Before mapping what to avoid and what to chase, the territory has to be clear. THRIVE is not a consumer social app. It is not a crypto project. It is not a design agency. It is a **permissioned operating portal** for finance and insurance, built around multi-agent orchestration, strategic intelligence (Old HQ), and field operations. The people wearing these portraits are operators — they run signal desks, coordinate banker runners, and govern an access-controlled system where every action is audited.

The visual identity therefore needs to communicate: **authority without vanity, intelligence without coldness, operational discipline without corporate sterility.** The palette — dark backgrounds, violet as the dominant accent, coral for warmth, gold for signal/status, mint for system health — is already distinctive. The portraits should feel like they belong on the bridge of a vessel, not on a LinkedIn carousel.

---

## II. Avoid List — Cliches That Kill Distinctiveness

### A. The "AI Filter Over a Headshot" Look
The single most common failure mode. A real photograph run through a style transfer or an img2img pipeline that softens skin, adds a faint glow, and otherwise looks like every conference speaker's profile. **This communicates nothing except "we tried AI once."**

### B. Bored Ape / CryptoPunk / PFP-Collection Derivatives
Flat cartoon characters with randomized trait layers (hats, glasses, backgrounds). Even if the execution is good, the formal language instantly reads "NFT drop." THRIVE is not a token project. This includes: pixel art faces, flat-color animal characters with accessories, and the "generative art club membership badge" genre broadly.

### C. Stock Photography + Gradient Overlay
A real or AI-generated person, competent but generic, placed on a violet-to-coral gradient background. This is what happens when someone takes the THRIVE palette literally but does nothing with it.

### D. Over-Saturated Vaporwave / Synthwave Generic
Neon grids, chrome text, sunset gradients, Roman busts. The aesthetic peaked around 2018-2020 and now reads as nostalgic decoration rather than forward-looking intelligence.

### E. "Hacker in a Hoodie" / Generic Cyberpunk
Dark environments, green terminal text reflected on faces, circuit-board overlays, binary rain. This is the visual shorthand for "tech" that Hollywood established and LinkedIn inherited.

### F. Hyper-Realistic AI Portraits That Try to Pass as Photographs
Faces generated to look indistinguishable from camera photographs. They also age poorly — as detection tools improve, they start looking evasive rather than creative. A portrait system should be visibly authored, not camouflaged.

### G. Any Recognizable Living Artist's Style
Do not replicate the specific manner of any identifiable working artist. Influence is fine; imitation is not. The test: if a knowledgeable viewer's first reaction is "that looks like [artist name]'s work," it is too close.

### H. Flat Corporate Illustration (the "Alegria" Family)
The rounded, disproportionate-limbed, pastel-colored human figures used by Facebook/Meta, Google, Slack, and dozens of enterprise SaaS products. Sometimes called "Corporate Memphis." These portraits are designed to be inoffensive and are therefore identity-free.

## III. Inspiration Territory — Visual Lanes Worth Exploring

### Lane 1: "Strategic Cartography" — The Map Room Aesthetic
**Core idea:** Portraits that feel like they emerged from the same world as a war-room map table — layered, annotated, measured. Topographic contour lines as facial structure. Coordinate grids as compositional scaffolding. The person as terrain to be read, not just seen.

**Why it works for THRIVE:** Old HQ is literally a strategic intelligence layer. The language of cartography — surveys, waypoints, overlays, contour analysis — maps directly onto what the platform does.

**References to study (without copying):** Military topographic maps. Architectural section drawings. The visual language of sonar/radar displays. Renaissance anatomical diagrams.

### Lane 2: "Signal Intelligence" — The Waveform Portrait
**Core idea:** Faces built from or dissolving into signal data — oscilloscope traces, frequency spectra, waveform envelopes. Not "digital" in the cliche sense of binary code, but "signal" in the operational sense of pattern detection and analysis.

**Why it works for THRIVE:** The platform processes signals — financial, operational, strategic. A portrait that looks like it was captured by an instrument rather than a camera communicates that the person is part of a system that reads the world as data.

**References to study:** Medical imaging (MRI cross-sections, ultrasound). Seismograph output. Radio telescope visualizations. Audio spectrograms rendered as landscapes.

### Lane 3: "The Composed Operator" — Restrained Figurative Painting
**Core idea:** Portraits in the tradition of formal portraiture — composed, deliberate, background as context — but executed with a visible digital hand. Not photorealistic, not cartoon. The kind of painting that would hang in the boardroom of an institution that takes itself seriously but is not stuck in the 19th century.

**Why it works for THRIVE:** Finance and insurance are old industries. The operational layer is new. A portrait style that bridges formal tradition and contemporary tool-making says "we respect the weight of what we manage, and we build new ways to manage it."

**References to study:** John Singer Sargent's environmental portraits. Gerhard Richter's photo-paintings. The muted palette and structured compositions of Vilhelm Hammershoi.

### Lane 4: "Operational Overlay" — The HUD Portrait
**Core idea:** A portrait with a transparent information layer — subtle UI elements, status indicators, coordinate marks — as if the person is being viewed through a heads-up display or an operator's console.

**Why it works for THRIVE:** The platform IS an operational console. Portal members are operators with roles, capabilities, and audit trails. The HUD layer communicates that these are people who exist within a system — and the system acknowledges them.

**References to study:** Real military CIC (Combat Information Center) displays. Bloomberg terminal typography and layout. NASA mission control interfaces. Dieter Rams's design language applied to data display.

### Lane 5: "Architectural Identity" — The Blueprint Portrait
**Core idea:** Portraits rendered in the visual language of technical drawing — precise linework, annotation, dimensional notation, section cuts. The person as a structure to be understood.

**Why it works for THRIVE:** The codebase itself is architecturally rigorous — role-capability matrices, migration paths, access control that fails closed. A blueprint aesthetic mirrors the engineering discipline of the platform.

**References to study:** Cyanotype printing process. Technical patent drawings. Exploded-view diagrams. Anatomical illustrations from Gray's Anatomy.

## IV. Look-Alike Risk Map

### High Risk (Would Be Mistaken For)

| Collection / Style | Why It Is Too Close | Boundary Line |
|---|---|---|
| **World of Women (WoW) NFT collection** | Colorful illustrated portraits with diverse subjects and bold palettes | Keep brushwork visible. Avoid flat-fill color regions. Never randomize accessories. |
| **Azuki / Anime-derivative PFP sets** | Any portrait with clean linework, large eyes, and a "character select screen" feel | Maintain architectural precision in lines. No stylized eyes. No "character" energy. |
| **AI Yearbook / Epik App trend (2023-2024)** | AI-generated portraits styled as retro yearbook photos | Stay clearly non-photographic. The hand of the tool should be visible. |
| **Midjourney "default aesthetic" (v5-v6 era)** | Hyper-detailed, slightly airbrushed, dramatic lighting | Avoid unearned drama. Every visual decision should serve the identity, not spectacle. |
| **Lensa AI "Magic Avatars" (2022-2023)** | Saturated, fantastical, personality-free | No fantasy elements. No "character class" energy. These are operators, not avatars. |

### Medium Risk (Could Be Confused With, at a Glance)

| Collection / Style | Differentiation Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Obvious "boardroom portrait" AI generation** | Ground in the specific THRIVE palette. Add the operational layer. Never let the background be a generic blur. |
| **Data visualization art** | The portrait must be of a *person*, not of data shaped like a person. Data elements support the portrait; they do not replace it. |
| **Glitch art portraits** | If using signal/waveform elements, keep them structured and intentional. Glitch art is deliberately chaotic; THRIVE's signal processing is deliberately ordered. |
| **Cyberpunk 2077 / Blade Runner derivative aesthetics** | Differentiate by avoiding: rain, neon signage, dystopian environmental cues, and the cyan-magenta signature pairing. |

### Low Risk (Shared DNA but Distinguishable)

| Adjacent Territory | Why It Is Safe to Be Near |
|---|---|
| **Bauhaus graphic design** | Geometric structure, restrained palette, functional composition. Good DNA to share. Distinguishable because Bauhaus rarely does portraiture. |
| **Swiss/International typographic style** | Grid discipline, hierarchy through scale, restrained color. The overlay elements can borrow this grammar without being mistaken for it. |
| **Satellite / aerial imagery aesthetics** | The cartographic lane shares some visual DNA with processed satellite imagery. The domains are different enough that the echo reads as sophistication, not copying. |

## V. The Distinctive Space

Synthesizing the above, the unique visual territory for THRIVE portraits sits at an intersection that no existing collection occupies:

**Operational Portraiture** — portraits of people who exist within and are defined by a working system. Not characters, not avatars, not headshots. Operators. The visual language borrows from:

- **Cartography and technical drawing** (structure, precision, annotation)
- **Signal processing and instrumentation** (waveforms, spectra, measurement)
- **Formal portraiture tradition** (composition, gravity, context)
- **Operational interface design** (HUD elements, status, system awareness)

The palette is locked: **dark field, violet structure, coral warmth, gold signal, mint status.** These five values are the color identity.

The single most important differentiator: **these portraits should look like they were made by a system that takes its operators seriously.** Not playful, not ironic, not decorative. Purposeful. The way a well-designed instrument panel is beautiful — because every element is there for a reason.

## VI. Decision Checklist for Downstream Agents

Before finalizing any portrait, apply these tests:

1. **The Screenshot Test:** If this portrait appeared as a 48px avatar in a portal sidebar, would it still read as intentional and distinct?
2. **The Collection Test:** If you saw all 10 portraits together, would they clearly belong to the same system without looking identical?
3. **The Association Test:** Does this portrait make you think of any specific existing collection, app, or trend before you think of THRIVE?
4. **The Gravity Test:** Does this portrait communicate that the person wearing it makes consequential decisions?
5. **The Tool Test:** Is the fact that this was made with AI visible and intentional, rather than hidden or accidental?
