## 1. CHARACTER ARCHETYPE: The Strategos

These ten figures are **Strategoi** — a term borrowed from the ancient Greek for generals who held both tactical command and political authority. In the THRIVE universe, a Strategos is a decision-architect: someone who sits at the convergence of structured data, live signals, and human judgment, and synthesizes them into action.

They are not coders. They are not analysts in the narrow sense. They are the people who occupy the chair when the signal arrives and the window is closing — the ones trusted to read a dashboard of contradictory indicators and commit. Think of them as **operational intelligence officers for a world where finance, insurance, and strategic foresight have merged into a single discipline.**

Each Strategos specializes differently, but they share a posture: **composed intensity**. They are never frantic, never passive. Their faces carry the particular calm of someone who has already processed the crisis and is now choosing the response. Eyes are engaged. Jaws are set but not clenched. There is authority without aggression.

**The unifying trait:** Every portrait should read as a person you would trust with a consequential decision at 2 AM — someone whose competence is bone-deep and whose composure is earned, not performed.

| # | Title | Domain | Energy |

## 2. SETTING: The Operations Floor at Old HQ

The portraits exist in a single implied location: **the Operations Floor of Old HQ** — THRIVE's strategic intelligence layer rendered as a physical space.

### What Old HQ Looks Like

Old HQ is not a Silicon Valley open-plan office. It is not a military bunker. It sits between those ideas — a **high-ceilinged operations room with the materiality of a mid-century intelligence center and the information density of a modern trading floor**, but quieter. Think: the visual language of a well-funded observatory crossed with a situation room.

**Key spatial characteristics:**

- **The light comes from the data.** The primary illumination source in every portrait is ambient light cast by unseen displays, holographic projections, or data surfaces. This gives faces a cool, slightly unnatural luminosity — lit from below-center or from the side by information itself.

- **Materials are heavy and real.** Where background surfaces are visible, they are dark brushed metal, matte concrete, smoked glass, or deep wood — nothing plastic, nothing disposable. Old HQ was built to last. It has weight.

- **Depth is atmospheric, not architectural.** Backgrounds do not show full rooms. They show depth through atmosphere: soft bokeh of distant indicator lights, the gentle glow of a status board out of focus, the suggestion of a large dark space beyond the figure. The Strategos is always in the foreground, and the world recedes behind them into productive darkness.

- **There is no daylight.** Old HQ operates outside the diurnal cycle. The palette is nocturnal. If there is warmth, it comes from a gold or amber accent — a signal light, a reflection — never from sunlight.

### The Emotional Texture of the Space

Old HQ feels like **the hour between the signal arriving and the decision being made**. It is not tense — tension implies uncertainty about whether things will hold. It is *gravid* — full, weighted, on the edge of resolution. The space hums. Systems are running. The Strategos is ready.

## 3. VISUAL LORE: Recurring Elements and Symbols

### 3.1 The THRIVE Chromatic System

Every portrait is built on the platform's five-color palette, used with discipline:

| Color | Hex | Role in Portraits |
|-------|-----|-------------------|
| **Deep Ink** | `#16142b` | The dominant ground. Backgrounds, shadows, and negative space are built from deep ink and its near-neighbors. This is the gravity well of the palette. |
| **Violet** | `#6d42e5` | The signature accent. Used for the most important visual element in each portrait beyond the face itself — a rim light, a reflected data surface, a geometric element. Violet = THRIVE's presence. |
| **Coral** | `#ff7062` | The alert accent. Used sparingly — a single indicator light, a status glow, a warm edge on one side of the face. Coral means something is live, active, demanding attention. |
| **Gold** | `#f4bd4f` | The trust accent. Used for the warmest light in the portrait — a reflection in the eyes, a soft fill on one cheekbone, the color of a data element that represents a positive signal. Gold = earned confidence. |
| **Mint** | `#58c7a2` | The system accent. Used for ambient data-light — the glow of a secondary display, a status-healthy indicator, the color of the operational background hum. Mint = systems nominal. |

**Critical rule:** No portrait uses all five accents at full intensity. Each portrait features **deep ink as the base, violet as the primary accent, and exactly two of the remaining three as secondary accents.** This creates family resemblance while allowing each portrait its own chromatic character.

### 3.2 The Data Veil

Every portrait includes what I am calling **the Data Veil** — a layer of faint, semi-transparent data visualization elements that floats between the viewer and the subject, or between the subject and the background. This is the collection's most distinctive visual signature.

The Data Veil is NOT a HUD overlay slapped on a photo. It is integrated into the portrait's lighting and atmosphere. Think of it as **information made architectural** — as if the air in Old HQ is lightly saturated with projected data, and the portrait captures a figure standing inside that field.

**Data Veil elements may include:**
- Fine geometric grid lines (not a full grid — fragments, fading at the edges)
- Small node-and-edge graph fragments (referencing THRIVE's multi-agent topology)
- Subtle radial or concentric patterns (referencing signal processing)
- Thin horizontal scan lines at very low opacity
- Small clusters of dots or micro-typography (too small to read, implying data density)

**Data Veil rules:**
- Always rendered in one of the five palette colors at 15-30% opacity
- Never obscures the face — it may cross the background, shoulders, or edges of the frame, but the face reads clean through it
- Each portrait uses a different Veil pattern. No two are identical.

### 3.3 The Sigil

Each portrait contains a single **Sigil** — a small, precise geometric mark that functions as a role identifier. It is positioned consistently in the lower-right quadrant of the frame (or reflected subtly in a surface near the subject).

The Sigil is abstract and geometric — not a logo, not a pictogram. It is built from the same visual language as the Data Veil but rendered at full opacity and at a smaller scale. Think of it as a rank insignia or a department mark.

**Sigil construction rules:**
- Built from circles, lines, and angles only — no curves beyond the circle
- Rendered in violet (`#6d42e5`) with a faint glow
- Approximately 5-8% of the frame's shorter dimension in size
- Unique per portrait, but all ten Sigils should feel like they belong to the same alphabet

### 3.4 The Reflection Point

Every Strategos has one point on their face or figure where **gold light catches** — a small, warm highlight that reads as reflected data. It might be in one eye, on the bridge of the nose, along a jawline, on a collar. This is the "soul point" of each portrait: the single warm note in the cool field that says *this is a person, not a system.*

## 4. COHERENCE RULES: What Makes Every Portrait "Part of This Collection"

These rules are non-negotiable. Any portrait that breaks them is out of family.

### 4.1 Composition

- **Framing:** Bust composition — head and upper shoulders. No full torso, no hands, no mid-body crops. The face is the subject.
- **Aspect ratio:** Square (1:1). These are profile pictures; they must work in circular crops without losing the face.
- **Face position:** The subject's face occupies the central 40-60% of the frame vertically. Eyes sit on or slightly above the horizontal center line.
- **Head angle:** Between 0 degrees (facing camera) and 25 degrees off-axis. No dramatic three-quarter turns, no profiles. The Strategos meets the viewer's gaze or nearly does.
- **Gaze:** Eyes are open and directed at or near the camera. No closed eyes, no dramatic upward gazes, no looking away. The Strategos acknowledges you.

### 4.2 Color Temperature

- **Overall:** Cool-dominant. The portrait's average color temperature sits between 5500K and 7500K (daylight to overcast). It never feels warm overall.
- **Warm accents are earned, not ambient.** Gold and coral appear as point sources or reflections, never as a wash.
- **Background to subject gradient:** The background is always darker and cooler than the subject. The figure emerges from darkness, not from light.

### 4.3 Lighting

- **Key light:** Soft, directional, coming from approximately 45 degrees to one side and slightly below center. This is the "data-glow" light — cool-toned, slightly violet or mint-shifted.
- **Fill:** Minimal. The shadow side of the face is allowed to go deep. Contrast ratio between lit and shadow side should be at least 3:1.
- **Rim/edge light:** Present on the shadow side — a thin violet or mint line separating the figure from the background. This rim light is the single most important coherence element. Every portrait has it.
- **No overhead lighting.** No top-down key. The light in Old HQ comes from the sides and from below, because that is where the screens and data surfaces are.

### 4.4 Background Treatment

- **Base:** Deep ink (`#16142b`) or a color within two steps of it on the value scale.
- **Gradient:** A subtle radial gradient centered slightly off the subject's head — lighter near the figure, darker at the edges. This creates a natural vignette.
- **Data Veil elements** (see Section 3.2) provide texture and break the flatness.
- **No environmental detail.** No furniture, no walls, no windows, no objects. The background is atmosphere and data, not architecture.

### 4.5 Level of Abstraction

- **Realism gradient:** These portraits sit at approximately 70-80% photorealism. Faces are recognizable, anatomically correct, and emotionally readable. But they are not photographs — there is a painterly quality, a slight stylization in how light and shadow resolve. Skin has texture but not pores. Eyes have depth but not bloodshot veins.
- **The uncanny zone to avoid:** Do not go fully photorealistic (it reads as "AI trying to fake a photo") or fully illustrated (it reads as "avatar, not portrait"). The sweet spot is **digital painting by a concept artist who trained on Rembrandt and Syd Mead**.

### 4.6 Emotional Register

- **Expression range:** Neutral to subtly determined. No smiles, no frowns, no drama. The Strategos is not performing emotion; they are present and engaged.
- **Intensity is in the eyes.** The rest of the face is composed; the eyes carry the energy.

## 5. VARIATION RULES: How the Ten Differ While Staying in Family

### 5.1 What Changes

| Dimension | Variation Range |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Gender presentation** | Full spectrum. At least 4 of the 10 should read as women, at least 3 as men, and at least 1 should be deliberately ambiguous. |
| **Apparent age** | 28 to 60. No one under 25 (too junior for the chair) or visibly over 65. |
| **Ethnicity** | Deliberately global. No more than 2 of the 10 should share the same broad ethnic presentation. |
| **Secondary accent pair** | Each portrait uses violet + exactly 2 of {coral, gold, mint}. All three combinations must appear across the set. |
| **Key light side** | Alternate left and right across the set. Approximately 5 from each side. |
| **Data Veil pattern** | Each portrait gets a unique Veil motif. No two alike. |
| **Sigil** | Each portrait gets a unique geometric mark. |
| **Clothing suggestion** | Minimal but varied. Dark-toned and understated. |
| **Hair** | Full variety — lengths, textures, styles, including shaved/bald. |

### 5.2 What Stays Fixed

These elements are identical across all ten portraits:

- Square 1:1 aspect ratio
- Bust framing with face centered
- Deep ink background base
- Violet as primary accent color
- Rim/edge light on shadow side
- Data Veil layer (pattern varies, presence does not)
- Sigil in lower-right quadrant
- Gold reflection point on face/figure
- Cool overall color temperature
- 70-80% photorealism level
- No environmental objects
- Gaze toward or near camera
- Expression in the neutral-to-determined range

### 5.3 Sequencing Suggestion

If the ten portraits are ever displayed in sequence, they should feel like a **slow pan across the Operations Floor** — each face emerging from the same darkness, lit by the same invisible systems, distinguished by their own accent color pair and Data Veil signature.

The emotional arc across the sequence should move from **alert stillness** (Portrait 01, The Founder) to **settled gravity** (Portrait 10, The Anchor).

## 6. PROMPT ENGINEERING NOTES FOR DOWNSTREAM AGENTS

When generating these portraits with an image model, the following language patterns should be consistent across all ten prompts:

**Always include:**
- "digital portrait painting, bust composition, square format"
- "deep ink background (#16142b), cool color temperature"
- "violet (#6d42e5) rim light on [left/right] side"
- "faint semi-transparent data visualization overlay"
- "eyes directed at viewer, composed expression"
- "70-80% photorealism, concept art quality"
- "no text, no logos, no hands visible"

**Never include:**
- "photograph" or "photo" (pushes too realistic)
- "cartoon" or "anime" (pushes too stylized)
- "smile" or "happy" (wrong register)
- "bright" or "daylight" or "sunny" (wrong lighting paradigm)
- "busy background" or any named location (violates background rules)
